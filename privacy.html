Where I get your data, and what I collect

There are three different ways I might hold data about you, and they work differently. This section says which applies to you.

1. If I contacted you first

If you received an email from me without asking for one, your details were collected from publicly accessible sources — your firm's own website, its public Google Maps business listing, and the public register at Companies House. I did not buy a list, and your details did not come from a data broker.

What I hold is limited to business contact information: firm name, business email address, business website, town or city, publicly listed company number and director name, and publicly visible information about the services the firm offers.

Lawful basis: legitimate interests (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)) — my interest in contacting businesses that may benefit from a relevant service. I have assessed this as proportionate because contact is limited to business addresses at registered companies, the message relates to that firm's actual operations, volumes are low, and every message carries a plain way to opt out. Under PECR, marketing email to corporate subscribers is permitted where a clear opt-out is offered.

You can object at any time and I will stop. See Your rights.

2. If you contacted me

If you book a call or email me, I hold your name, email address, the firm you work for, and whatever you choose to tell me about your business — including your answers to the questions on the booking form.

Lawful basis: legitimate interests, and where we go on to work together, performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b)).

3. If you used a system I built for one of my clients

If you interacted with an assistant or enquiry form I built for a client of mine, that client is the data controller, not me. I act as a data processor on their instructions under a written agreement. Their privacy notice governs that data, and a request about it should go to them — though I will help them answer it.

Your rights

Under the UK GDPR you have the right to:

  • Be informed — which is what this notice is for
  • Access a copy of the personal data I hold about you
  • Rectification — have inaccurate data corrected
  • Erasure — ask me to delete your data
  • Restrict processing — ask me to pause using it
  • Data portability — receive your data in a portable format
  • Object — including an absolute right to object to direct marketing, which I must always honour
  • Rights around automated decision-making — I make no decisions with legal or similarly significant effects by automated means alone

To exercise any of these, email jack@jmburdett.com. I will respond within one month, free of charge. Replying "no", "stop" or "unsubscribe" to any email from me is enough — you do not need to mention this notice.
